The chapter in 60 seconds

A foundation takes tower loads into the ground without unacceptable movement. Everything starts with knowing the soil, by borings and probes at each site. Compact foundations resist a moment with one body: monoblock, slab, or single pile. Separate foundations resist tension and compression under each leg: stepped blocks, auger-bored shafts, grillages or piles. Uplift is usually the governing check, and the earth frustum method stands in for shear resistance. Tests prove design, and on piles often every unit.

Why it matters

Soil varies along every line, so a single design does not fit. Foundations should stay stronger than towers, because a foundation failure is hard to reach and hard to repair.

13.1Requirements and preconditionsp. 472

Foundations carry each load case into the subsoil with sufficient reliability, and without inadmissible movement. Support loads, site survey and soil investigation are the three inputs.

13.2Types of subsoilp. 473–475

Soil by particle size

Residual soils stay where formed and are generally firm. Transferred soils, mostly glacial, can be soft to great depth. Rock depends on weathering. Fill is usable only when well compacted; rubbish tips are not.

13.3Subsoil investigationp. 475–487

Investigate each site, and always where peat, mud, silt or quick sand may occur. In uniform terrain, designs for normal, weak and very good soil can be chosen at excavation.

Foundation types

Monoblock without base enlargement

Slab foundation: overturning and bearing

Slab reinforcement can follow a beam grillage, or better a slab model using the legs as supports, avoiding stirrups. Bearing distribution: conventional (linear), subgrade modulus (s = σ/Ks), or compressibility modulus.

Uplift with an earth frustum

Stepped blocks

  • Type A: bottom step against undisturbed soil, firm dry ground.
  • Type S: all cast against planking, loose or wet ground.
  • Extra: G/S ≥ 0.67 (A) or 0.80 (S) characteristic.
  • Step height to projection n ≥ 1.4, unreinforced.

Other separate types

  • Auger-bored: firm dry cohesive, vertical shaft, bell at base.
  • Grillage: steel pyramid 2 to 4 m, no concrete, needs good compacted backfill. Uplift factors 1.5 to 1.8.
  • Piles: high water or deep bearing layer; factor 2.0 characteristic, 1.5 ultimate for theoretical uplift.

Single pile by Blum

Leg anchoring checks concrete compression, bond and weld, as in 7.50, 1.84 and 136 N/mm² in the book's example. Guyed towers: central footing in compression, anchors in uplift, 2.5 m deep in good soil, 3.5 m in poor soil. Field test prototypes. German rule: γM = 1.65 on guy anchorage. Timber: bury one sixth of length, at least 1.6 m.
