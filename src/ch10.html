The chapter in 60 seconds

Fittings attach, connect and protect. Suspension and dead-end clamps hold the conductor, connectors join it, spacers keep bundles apart, and dampers take the energy out of vibration. Insulator fittings link everything to the tower and guide arcs and fields. Each fitting must not run hotter than the conductor, must not stress it where it is already most stressed, and must survive years of vibration without loosening.

Why it matters

A conductor is most stressed at its clamps. A fitting costs little, but its failure drops a conductor or starts fatigue that spreads. This chapter links insulators, conductors and vibration.

10.1Definitionsp. 305

Fittings for conductors

Terminate, suspend or join: suspension and dead-end clamps, connectors, branch clamps, dampers, spacers.

Fittings for insulator sets

Between the clamp assembly and the first detachable part at the support: yoke plates, corona rings, U-bolts, links.

10.2Fittings for conductorsp. 305–312

Inside a clamp, four stresses overlap: tension, static bending, clamping compression and alternating bending from vibration (Fig. 10.1). A clamp should be light and pivoted so it can follow the motion, with its pivot near the conductor axis.

Suspension clamps

Dead-end terminations

Turnbuckles adjust lengths in dead-end sets. They must not be loaded by bending, and their thread needs extra partial factors. Connectors may be tension-proof, like compression joints, or non-tension-proof, like jumper clamps. Aluminium with copper needs a bimetal sheet, or better an Al/Cu clamp, against galvanic corrosion.

Repairing a damaged conductor

Spacer plan

Stockbridge dampers are a spring-mass system tuned across the wide frequency range of vortex vibration, sized with the energy balance principle. Spacer dampers use rubber hinges, each with as much damping as about 100 m of conductor. Phase spacers do not stop galloping, but greatly reduce flashovers and clashing. Spacers are not earthing points in maintenance, because hinges and insulating elements do not conduct.

10.4Rating and testsp. 313–317

Current-carrying fittings may not exceed conductor temperature or have a higher resistance than an equal length of conductor. Current density is tolerated up to 70 A/mm² in tension-loaded and 80 A/mm² in other fittings. Non-magnetic materials cut eddy current heating.

Mechanical checks

Corona test voltage

Iron parts are hot-dip galvanised. Stainless steel is used for bolts, aluminium alloys for clamps and armor rods, EPDM or silicone for damping. Materials must not be prone to stress crack corrosion. Type tests prove the design, including fatigue of elastomers. Sample tests prove the batch. Routine tests are non-destructive on tension-loaded parts.
