The chapter in 60 seconds

A finished line is checked, tested and handed over, then earns its keep for decades. Monitoring squeezes more from it: real-time ratings, lightning location, ice and galloping alerts. Asset management turns decisions into numbers, comparing the planned cost and risk of each option by net present value. Maintenance rests on inspection, from a patrol to full-scale testing. Failures are investigated without prejudging the cause. Availability is measured in hours, and old lines can be refurbished, upgraded or uprated instead of replaced.

Why it matters

Lines are hard to build and last 50 years or more. In the book's example, spending 26 000 EUR on two targeted measures cuts annual cost and risk from 294 898 to 136 582 EUR. A 1 000 000 EUR second line does worse.

17.2Operation and monitoringp. 686–693

Thermal rating is the current that keeps the conductor at its design temperature, where legal clearance is just met. Fixed ratings use cautious weather assumptions. Real-time monitoring shows the true margin, without running hotter than designed. Losses rise near the limit, so it is not for continuous use.

Combining weather and tension gets the benefit of both. Knowing true ampacity may delay dispatch changes after a disturbance, and may postpone upgrades.

Lightning location times the wave at many sensors, synchronised by GPS. It warns crews, speeds fault finding and helps future design. Lightning makes up to 30 % of faults in Germany and 60 % in Brazil.

Ice monitoring triggers countermeasures. Hydro-Québec's system uses 20 sites, and load cells can show ice as well.

Galloping readiness

Insulators. Cap-and-pin strings are checked by voltage across each unit, never in wet weather. A spark-gap tester gives no sound from a dead disc. Long rods are puncture-proof and need no test. Composite units have a critical defect length of 35 to 40 % of design length, so they need periodic diagnosis.

Central Europe flies about once a year at about 20 km/h, patrols once a year, and inspects in depth about every ten years. Helicopters cover up to 300 km a day and look down on the line, but cannot fly over dense urban areas.

Which tool finds what

Foundation inspection levels

Internal conductor corrosion cannot be seen until late. It shows as bulging, because corrosion products take up several times the volume of aluminium. A joint is critical when its resistance reaches two or three times that of an equal conductor length. Normally a sound joint runs cooler than the conductor.

Glass may fail early then settles. Porcelain has few early faults but ages at a growing rate, mainly by pin corrosion. Long rods need only visual checks. Use two or more methods on composites.

Ways of working

Coating is renewed every 10 to 20 years. Right-of-way cycles run three years in the tropics and five to ten in moderate climates. RWE breaks up clear-cutting with a stepped forest edge, slow-growing species, chess board, fishbone or island plots, and no herbicides.

17.4.4Investigating failuresp. 719–723

Do not judge early. First rule out what cannot have happened, then test several scenarios against the data, as a team. Reach the site before repair erases evidence. Rust on a break shows an older crack, burn marks show a discharge, and loose bolts may mean sabotage.

Failures by cause

Most failures had causes that can be treated statistically, and most lines were designed deterministically. Few were foundation failures. Observed order of failure: suspension towers first, then hardware, then foundations. In the conductor system: insulators, hardware, conductors.

17.6Refurbishment, upgrading, upratingp. 727–731

Three routes

Raising design temperature from 50 to 80 °C gives about 30 % more power, but needs a full redesign for clearances: new survey, higher towers, midspan towers or restringing. Laser scanning helps. Voltage or conductor changes bring new loads, so check the structure.

Ruhr conversion

Aim of upgrading

Weser crossing: leg load rose from 2350 to 2965 kN. Two high-tensile angles were added to the two existing ones, joined with batten plates at 700 mm. Open angle-section towers can be reinforced at acceptable cost, unlike other supports. Ice upgrade: block foundations of five tension towers took 320 kN more, by adding a slab between the two uplift-loaded blocks.
