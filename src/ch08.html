The chapter in 60 seconds

Earth wires shield conductors from lightning, return earth fault current, reduce interference and spare the substation earthing mat. In normal service they carry almost nothing, so their size is set by short circuits: heating so fast that no heat escapes. The rating follows from an adiabatic formula and a temperature limit. Many utilities now use larger conductors near substations and steel further out. With optical fibres, the earth wire also became a telecommunications link: OPGW.

Why it matters

Short-circuit levels keep rising, and a wire sized for today may sag or weaken tomorrow. Selection needs to look at least ten years ahead, and sag must be coordinated with the conductors below.

What an earth wire does

Older lines used steel or low-aluminium ACSR. Higher fault levels moved many utilities, especially in Europe, to larger ACSR or other conductive cables. A common compromise is high conductivity for the first 5 to 10 km from a substation, then steel.

8.3Mechanical designp. 250–251

Overheating can elongate, weaken or break a wire, and permanent stretch reduces separation from phases.

Sag rise of steel wire, 400 m span

Earth wire sag is usually set to 90 to 95 % of conductor sag at the mean yearly temperature (10 °C in Europe), never exceeding the conductor sag, so that midspan separation is at least that at the tower.
