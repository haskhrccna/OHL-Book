The chapter in 60 seconds

A line is a chain of components, and one failure can cost the whole transfer capacity. Modern design treats loads and strengths as statistics: a load with return period Tr meets a strength with a 10 % exclusion limit, which gives a yearly failure probability between 1/Tr and 1/(2Tr). Strengths are then coordinated so the failure you can live with happens first: a suspension tower. Wind, ice and their combination come from measured statistics through standards that differ, sometimes a lot.

Why it matters

Loads drive tower weight, foundation size and conductor tension, so they drive cost. The choice of reliability level and wind model changes tower moments by more than 20 % in the book's example.

System reliability is the product of component reliabilities. When one component is far less reliable than the others, it alone sets the system value. Ice and wind loads follow an extreme value distribution such as Gumbel. Strengths follow normal or log-normal distributions.

Three reliability levels apply in EN 50341-1, by return period. Level 1, 50 years, for lines up to 150 kV. Level 2, 150 years, for lines from 230 kV that are the main or only supply. Level 3, 500 years, for interconnections, power plant connections and important consumers. Germany designs for 50 year loads with partial factors that reach the 500 year requirement.

6.2Strengths of componentsp. 157–159

The damage limit corresponds to the elastic limit, the failure limit to collapse or rupture. Characteristic strength Rc = R̄·(1 − Ue·vR), and Ue is 1.28 for 10 %, about 1.6 for 2 to 5 %, 2.1 under 2 %. The EN design value divides by a material factor: R = Rc/γM.

6.4Ice loadsp. 165–169

Precipitation icing gives glaze from freezing rain and wet snow. In-cloud icing gives soft and hard rime in the mountains. Lines crossing a valley collect more ice than those running along it.

6.6Climatic loads in the standardsp. 172–184

IEC 60826 is the world reference, EN 50341-1 the European one with National Normative Aspects (EN 50341-3). The standards give procedures, while national parts give numbers. The drag factor is 1.0 for conductors, 1.2 for insulators, and up to about 2.8 or more for lattice towers, depending on solidity ratio.

6.7Construction and maintenance loadsp. 185–186
  • IEC 60826: elements withstand twice lifting loads, or 1.5 times if well controlled.
  • Stringing: 1.5 × tension for stationary conductors, 2 × for moving ones.
  • Members people stand on: 1.5 kN vertical at mid-length in IEC, 1.0 kN with a 1.5 partial factor in EN.
  • EN: members inclined under 60° count as mountable.
  • Check temporary anchors and dead-ends for their vertical and longitudinal loads.
6.8Failure containmentp. 187–189

Security measures aim to limit cascades. IEC 60826 applies a residual static load from releasing one conductor at everyday tension without wind or ice, with suspension string swing allowed for. It also considers longitudinal loads, for example a 50 % difference in tension. Stronger measures: factor 1.5, several attachment points together on multi-circuit lines, and section towers about every tenth tower in icy areas.
